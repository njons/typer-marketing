## Why relevant
Guariglia's beat is AI in policing and surveillance - exactly the context where "software that collects nothing" is a meaningful counterpoint. He writes about systems that harvest data by design. Typer is the inverse: AI that is architecturally incapable of collecting anything. That framing (privacy by architecture, not policy) fits his analytical lens.

## Outreach notes
EFF staff writers don't need a pitch in the traditional sense - they find stories on their own. The right approach is a brief "here's a concrete example of privacy-by-design in AI software, thought it might be useful context" message. Not asking for coverage, offering a case study.

Lead with his 702 piece or AI policing angle. One sentence on what Typer is. One sentence on why it's architecturally different from every other AI (no account, no telemetry, not even web searches leave the device). Soft close.
